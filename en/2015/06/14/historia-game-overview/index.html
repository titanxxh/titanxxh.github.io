[titanxxh's Essen 2014] Game Overview 1: HISTORIA

titanxxh2015-06-142026-08-23

Essen 2014 has just ended, and HISTORIA received unanimous praise, taking first place on GeekBuzz. Yet most players who did not attend and try it in person—including me—may not know exactly what the game is about. Why is it so compelling? After reading the rulebook over the past two days, titanxxh decided to try a new series for the first time: titanxxh’s Essen 2014, covering games I purchased as well as introductions to several others. I welcome your feedback!

HISTORIA

Game Introduction

In HISTORIA, each player controls a civilization beginning in the late Stone Age, with the goal of carrying it from a tiny spark to glorious achievement. Your civilization will discover new technologies and expand into new territories. You can learn inventions by trading with neighboring, more advanced civilizations; exploit your own lands to obtain more resources for future development; or, of course, build military power and wage campaigns in every direction.
Building wonders will also elevate a civilization above its rivals and attract tourists later in the game. Leaders and national advisers assist you throughout.
The glory of victory belongs to the player who leaves the deepest mark on human history!

Components

  • 1 game board
  • 54 Power cubes
  • 45 Wonder cards (can you identify these three?)
  • 6 Development markers
  • 18 Leader cards (and these three?)
  • 16 Territory scoring tiles

  • 60 Action cards
  • 2 Time markers
  • 12 per-round scoring markers…
  • 30 Adviser cards
  • 7 Civilization cards
  • 6 Player aids

Game Board

Most of the board is self-explanatory after reading the rulebook, so I will not explain every element. The parts that do need explanation are:
The timeline: the inner ring represents eras and the outer ring represents rounds. There are three eras, each with four rounds. Why rounds vary in length will be explained later.
The Development Matrix: the vertical axis represents military strength and the horizontal axis technology.
Government bonuses: rewards are granted according to your type of government.

Setup

  1. Place the board in the center…
  2. Place one round marker on Era I and the other on Round 1.
  3. Separate the Wonder cards into three decks by era and shuffle them. Draw seven Era I cards to form the Wonder row; draw five in a two- or three-player game.
  4. Each player chooses a color, takes the four matching Power cubes, and places one in the Used Cube Pool.
  5. Each player takes ten Action cards and sets War and Tourism aside; they enter play later. The remaining eight cards form the player’s hand.
  6. Randomly assign a Civilization card and its five corresponding Adviser cards to each player. Each player shuffles their Adviser cards, places them face down, and draws one. (Players may also choose their civilizations.)
  7. Randomly determine turn order.
  8. Each player puts one marker on 0 of the score track and one in the lower-left corner of the Development Matrix.
  9. Randomly place the Territory scoring tiles on the world map.
  10. Separate the Leader cards into three decks by era and shuffle them. The player last in turn order draws a number of Leader cards equal to the player count, chooses one, and passes the rest to the next-to-last player. Continue until everyone has chosen, then reveal all Leaders together.
  11. Beginning with the starting player, each player places a Power cube in a territory and scores the points on its Territory tile.

Game Flow

The game consists of three eras, each with four rounds, for a total of twelve rounds.
At the end of a round, move the round marker clockwise to the next space and resolve its actions (described under End of the Round).

Victory Condition

At the end of the game, the player with the most points wins. Ties are broken in favor of the highest combined military and technology levels.

Game Rounds

Each round consists of several action cycles, depending on how many Action cards are played. In each action cycle, players decide which Action cards and/or Adviser cards to play from their hands.

Action Cards

To play Action cards, all players simultaneously choose one or more Action and/or Adviser cards, place them face down, and then reveal them together.
The maximum number of Action cards a player can play depends on the civilization’s technology level: initially one, two after discovering The Wheel, and three after discovering Medicine.

The following conditions apply when playing cards:

  • You must play at least one Action and/or Adviser card, even if it cannot be resolved.
  • Once revealed, cards cannot be withdrawn.
  • Some Action cards require Power cubes. You cannot use cubes in the Used Cube Pool; cubes spent from your supply enter that pool.
  • In turn order, each player resolves all cards they played.
  • During their turn, a player chooses the order in which to resolve their cards.
  • Some Action cards have an advanced effect, naturally at a higher cost.
  • Some Action cards have prerequisites; if they are not met, the card has no effect.

After an Action card is resolved, it is placed face up at the end of the discard queue. Adviser cards do not enter the discard queue; they are removed from the game.

Once every player’s cards have been resolved, begin a new action cycle. Continue until someone plays the Revolution Action card.

Action Descriptions

1. Military

Develop your civilization’s military strength. Spend one Power cube to gain one military level (move your marker one space upward on the Development Matrix).
Advanced effect (requires Metalworking): spend three Power cubes to gain two military levels.

  • If your civilization cannot increase its military level because of its technology level, the action has no effect. If you have reached the top of the Development Matrix (level 16), however, score 2 points.
  • A reward from a new military level may be used by your next action in the same action cycle.

2. Technology

Discover new technology. Spend one Power cube to gain one technology level (move your marker one space right on the Development Matrix).
Advanced effect (requires Science): spend three Power cubes to gain two technology levels.

  • If your civilization cannot increase its technology level because of its military level, the action has no effect. If you have reached the top of the Development Matrix (level 16), however, score 2 points.
  • A reward from a new technology level may be used by your next action in the same action cycle.

3. Art

Build a Wonder. Spend one Power cube to choose one Wonder from the Wonder row.
Advanced effect (requires Writing): spend three Power cubes to gain two Wonders.

  • A Wonder may be activated for its reward during the player’s actions, including on the next action of the same action cycle.
  • The Wonder row is not refilled immediately!

4. Exploit

Exploit your population. Return two Power cubes from the world map and/or your Used Cube Pool. If this makes you abandon a territory, lose points equal to that Territory tile’s value.
Advanced effect (requires Printing): lose 1 point to return four Power cubes.

  • If there are not enough cubes in the Used Cube Pool, simply return all available cubes.

5. Expansion

Expand your civilization. Place one Power cube in a territory adjacent to one you occupy.
Advanced effect (requires The Wheel): instead take one Power cube from your Used Cube Pool and place it on the world map.

  • A player with Navigation may expand into any territory.
  • When expanding into an empty territory, gain the reward on its Territory tile.
  • The United States and Russia are not adjacent (apparently the Bering Strait has vanished…), while Central and South America are adjacent! (The Panama Canal says hello.)

6. Trade

Trade with an adjacent civilization. It must have a higher technology level. You gain one technology level and it scores 2 points.
Advanced effect (requires Navigation): it scores only 1 point.

  • A civilization with Navigation can trade with any civilization.
  • Civilizations in the same territory count as adjacent…

7. Raid

Raid your neighbor. Your civilization may raid an adjacent civilization with a lower military level; remember that civilizations in the same territory count as adjacent.
Score 1 point and return one Power cube from the Used Cube Pool.
Advanced effect (requires Gunpowder): score 2 points and return two Power cubes from the Used Cube Pool.

  • If there is no adjacent civilization with a lower military level, the Raid has no effect.
  • You still score the points even if you cannot return every Power cube.

8. War

Declare war on another civilization in the same territory. War becomes available once your technology level is at least 2 (Agriculture).
The civilization with the higher military level wins and scores 2 points. The loser removes its Power cube from that territory and places it in the Used Cube Pool.
Advanced effect (requires Nuclear Power): the winner scores 4 points… (Is the game encouraging nuclear “peace”?)

  • Once a War action is played, it must be resolved even if that civilization can no longer win. (Once the arrow leaves the bow, there is no recalling it! Because actions resolve in sequence, a later player’s intended target may already have raised its military level.)
  • If military levels are tied, nothing happens; both sides stand down.
  • A player cannot lose their final territory.
  • If several civilizations share a territory, the attacker chooses which one to fight.

9. Tourism

Attract tourists to visit your Wonders. Tourism becomes available only after your civilization discovers Industrialization.
Score 1 point for every four Wonders you own.
Advanced effect (requires Internet): score 1 point for every three Wonders.

10. Revolution

This signals the end of a round. A Revolution Action card or an Adviser with a Revolution effect cannot be played if your discard queue contains fewer than three cards at the beginning of the action cycle. Revolution must be your final action.
Return the newest card in your discard queue (normally Revolution itself) and one other Action card to your hand.

When a player plays Revolution, move the round marker and begin the end-of-round phase.

End of the Round

At the end of each round, resolve every icon in that segment of the timeline ring. Icons in the large gray area are resolved individually by each player; icons in the small brown area affect other aspects of the game.
Resolve gray-area icons before brown-area icons.
Within the gray area, adding Power cubes must be resolved first; other icons may be resolved in any order.
Within the brown area, changing turn order must be resolved first.
Add Power cube: each player takes one Power cube from the common supply and places it in their Used Cube Pool.
Return Action cards: each player returns the two oldest Action cards from their discard queue to their hand.
Reset Wonders: each player readies any activated, sideways Wonder cards.
Return Power cubes: each player returns Power cubes from their Used Cube Pool; the number depends on how many territories they occupy.
Government bonus: each player gains the government bonus corresponding to their position on the Development Matrix.

Turn Order

Determine the new turn order from lowest score to highest. Among tied players, whoever acted later in the previous round acts earlier now. (A catch-up mechanism?)

Refill Wonders: discard all Wonders remaining in the Wonder row and draw seven new ones. When entering a new era, use that era’s Wonders.
Leader reward: every Leader has two conditions. If you satisfy either one, gain the reward; if you satisfy both, gain the higher reward. Whether or not the reward is earned, remove the Leader from the game.
Distribute Leaders: use the same process as during setup, but follow the newly determined turn order.
Territory reward: at the end of each era, if a territory contains only one civilization, that civilization scores the points on its Territory tile.
