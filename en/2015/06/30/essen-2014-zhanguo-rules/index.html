[titanxxh's Essen 2014] Game Overview 5—Zhanguo

titanxxh2015-06-302026-08-23

For the first installment after the return of my Essen 2014 series, I decided to translate the game that readers currently anticipate most and that I personally considered the most promising title of 2014: Zhanguo. At this point, I have to complain about foreigners’ understanding of Chinese language and history. According to the setting, the chaos of the Warring States period has already ended, and Qin Shi Huang has begun implementing measures to consolidate his rule and unify the empire. The game clearly should not be called Zhanguo; “Great Qin” or “Unification” would seem far more appropriate. Anyone expecting rival Warring States powers to fight one another may therefore be disappointed. Judging from the rules, however, Zhanguo continues What’s Your Game?’s tradition of heavy strategy titles. Fans of Eurogames should love it.


Zhanguo

Front of the box

Historical Background

Between 230 and 221 BCE, the ambitious young King Ying Zheng of Qin unified China and brought all the other Warring States under his rule.
The game takes place after this conquest. Although the King of Qin has proclaimed himself emperor, many occupied regions still do not consider themselves part of his empire. The peoples of the newly annexed states continue to regard their lands as independent kingdoms with their own traditions and laws, which they are unwilling to lose.
To prevent political disorder, Qin Shi Huang abolished the feudal system, introduced a system of commanderies and counties, divided China into several provinces, and personally appointed officials. He also decreed that differing cultural elements should be standardized to bind society together more effectively.
Domestic policy was not the only concern of the newly unified China. The Xiongnu posed a constant threat from the north, and Qin Shi Huang understood that the only way to remove that danger was to construct a great wall—one of the most extraordinary structures in human history.
In Zhanguo, players contribute to Qin Shi Huang’s project of unification. By completing a series of demanding tasks, they transform the divided states into a single empire.
The game lasts five rounds. In each round, players must make the best use of the cards they receive. A card may be placed on a player board to increase its owner’s personal influence within the empire, or played to the main board to recruit officials, construct palaces, move officials, appoint governors, or help build the Great Wall.
At the end of the game, the player who contributed most to Qin Shi Huang’s unification wins.

Components

  • 1 game board
  • 4 player boards
  • 120 cards: 40 ivory, 40 orange, and 40 brown
  • 40 worker tiles
  • 6 wall tiles
  • 12 task tiles
  • 1 round marker
  • 30 governors: 8 in each of four colors
  • 20 wall pieces: 5 in each of four colors
  • 24 palaces: 6 in each of four colors
  • 32 discs: 8 in each of four colors
  • 20 unrest markers
  • 60 officials: 20 red, 20 gray, and 20 white
  • 4 score tiles showing 100/200
  • 39 unification tokens in ivory, orange, and brown: twelve worth 1 point and one worth 3 points in each color

Setup

The illustration below shows the setup for a four-player game. With fewer than four players, remove the unused components.

  1. Game Board

    • Place the board in the center of the table. It is divided into several areas: rewards, tasks, court, provinces, and the Great Wall.

      • Provinces: the five provinces are identified by the following symbols.

      • Great Wall: the three Great Wall construction sites are identified by the following symbol.

  2. Player Supplies

    • Each player chooses a color—blue, red, purple, or yellow—and takes:
      • 8 discs
      • 8 governors
      • 6 palaces
      • 5 wall pieces
      • 5 unrest markers
      • 5 white officials
      • 5 gray officials
      • 5 red officials

  3. Cards

    • Separate the cards into three decks by the color on their backs and shuffle each deck.
    • Place the decks face down beside the board.

  4. Round Track

    • Place the round marker on space 1 of the round track.

  5. Three-Point Unification Tokens

    • Place the three tall octagonal columns in the court area of the board.

  6. First Player

    • Choose the first player randomly. Play proceeds clockwise.
    • In clockwise order, every player places one disc on the reward track, with the first player’s disc at the top.

    Note: The order of these discs changes every round. The player whose disc is at the top becomes first player, but turns still proceed clockwise from that player rather than following the order of the discs. The disc order matters only for breaking ties during the Unification Reward phase, described below.

  7. Victory Points

    • Every player places one disc on space 0 of the victory-point track.
      Victory points shown on a black background are awarded at the end of the game. Points shown on red are scored immediately.

  8. Task Discs

    • Every player places their remaining six discs beside the task area.

  9. Walls

    • Shuffle the six wall tiles and place one face up at each Great Wall construction site.

  10. Tasks

    • The task area is divided into two sections. Each section contains one Great Wall task, one palace task, and one governor task.
    • Shuffle the Great Wall tasks and place two face up in the task area. The other two are not used.
    • Repeat this procedure for the palace and governor tasks.

  11. General Supply

    • Place the worker tiles and one-point unification tokens beside the game board.

  12. Player Boards
    There are four player boards, identified by a circle, square, triangle, or star.
    Each board is double-sided. The symbols on side A are outlines, while those on side B are solid.
    All side-A boards are identical; every side-B board is different, as explained later.

    • We recommend using side A for your first few games.
    • If you decide to use side B, you may choose the boards through an auction or another method. Each player begins with 15 points.
      Note: Leave space above the player boards for cards.
      Each board is divided into five provinces, using the same symbols as the main board.
      Within every province, the government area is above and the worker area below.

  13. Officials

    • Every player takes the three officials indicated in province 1 of their player board and places them in province 1.

Note: With the exception of worker tiles, all resources are limited. Every component is visible to all players.

Round Overview

The game lasts five rounds. After the fifth, the player with the most victory points wins. Every round contains the following three phases:

  • Phase 1: Draw Cards
  • Phase 2: Play Cards
  • Phase 3: Unification Rewards

Draw Cards Phase

At the beginning of each round, every player draws two cards from each deck, giving them a hand of six cards with two of each color.

About the cards

During Phase 2, players use cards to perform actions.

Card fronts:
Every card has an ability, an activation number from 1 to 120, and a unification type: writing, currency, or law.
The ivory deck represents the unification of writing and contains cards 1–40.
The orange deck represents the unification of currency and contains cards 41–80.
The brown deck represents the unification of laws and contains cards 81–120.
Detailed card abilities are listed at the back of the rulebook.

Card backs:
The symbol in the center shows the card’s unification type.
The numbers below show the range of card numbers in the deck.
Players must allow their opponents to see the back of at least one card in their hand.

Play Cards Phase

Beginning with the first player and proceeding clockwise, each player plays one card to perform one action. Continue until everyone has played all six cards.

How to play a card
On a player’s turn, they may:

  • Perform a Unification Action by playing a card into one province of their player board.

or

  • Perform a Court Action by playing a card face up to the court area of the main board, covering the previously played card and forming a stack.

Unification Action

The player chooses a province and slides one card beneath the bottom edge of their player board, leaving the ability on its upper half visible.

Each province can hold at most three cards. Once placed, a card cannot be moved.

Unification tokens: the player takes one to three one-point unification tokens matching the type of the card just placed. The number depends on whether it is the first, second, or third card placed in that province.
If there are not enough one-point unification tokens, the player scores 1 victory point for each missing token.

Unrest markers: certain actions during the game increase unrest in a province. Every province has an unrest track that records its current level.

  • No unrest marker on the track represents unrest level 0.
  • Whenever the unrest level changes, move that province’s unrest marker.

Unification Actions and unrest: when performing a Unification Action, placing the second card in a province raises its unrest by 1, while placing the third raises it by 2. A player may not perform an action that would increase the province’s unrest beyond its maximum.

Court Action

The player places a card in the court area of the main board and performs one of the following six Court Actions.

Important! Any card may be used for any action.
Important! A player may choose only an action they can perform, and they must complete that action.

  • Recruit an Official

Every player’s personal supply contains five red, five white, and five gray cubes representing officials.
The player chooses one cube and places it in the government area of any province on their player board.
There is no limit to the number of cubes in a government area.

  • Move Officials

Officials may work in a government area or a worker area.
In a government area they carry out administrative duties; in a worker area they hire laborers for the emperor.
The Move Officials action lets a player move their officials to another province or between areas of the same province.
One movement point moves one official:
- From a government area to the government area of an adjacent province.
- From a worker area to the worker area of an adjacent province. A worker area may contain at most two officials.
- From a worker area to the government area of the same province.

Adjacent provinces share a solid border rather than a wall on the game board.

Important! Moving an official from a government area into the worker area of the same province costs no movement points and may be done at any time during your turn.

  • Appoint a Governor

The player chooses a province in which to appoint a governor, then:

  1. Removes one set of officials from that province on their player board and returns them to their personal supply.
  2. Places a governor in that province on the game board. The player may choose any unoccupied bonus space and immediately receives its bonus. If every bonus is occupied, place the governor beside them and receive no bonus.
  3. If the province’s unrest level is not 0, reduce it to 0.
  • Hire Workers

The player chooses a province, then:

  1. Hires workers according to the number of officials in that province’s worker area. Each official hires one worker.
    Because a worker area can contain at most two officials, this action can hire no more than two workers.
  2. Regardless of the number hired, increase that province’s unrest by 1. This action cannot be performed if the province is already at maximum unrest.
  • Build a Palace

The player chooses a province, then:

  1. Removes two workers from that province and one worker from a different province, returning them to the general supply.
  2. Places a palace in an empty palace space in the chosen province.
  3. If possible, takes one three-point unification token.
  4. Scores 1 point for every card currently tucked beneath their player board.

Important! A player may not build a palace in a province whose unrest level is too high.

  • Build a Wall

Three Great Wall construction sites are marked by flags on the map. Each site contains two wall sections, and each section has a wall tile assigned to it.
The player chooses a wall section, then:

  1. Places one wall piece in one of the section’s three wall spaces. A player may have no more than one wall piece among the three spaces in the same section.
  2. Removes one, two, or three workers according to the chosen wall space. Those workers must come from different provinces.

Activating Card Abilities

As noted earlier, any card can be used for any action. After completing the chosen Court Action, however, the card played may activate abilities on cards in the player’s board.
The player chooses the order of activation and receives the reward shown in the upper-right corner of each activated card.
Two conditions must be satisfied to activate an ability:

  • The number on the played card must meet the relevant requirement. For Hire Workers, Build a Palace, or Build a Wall, its number must be higher than the card previously on top of the court stack. For Appoint a Governor or Recruit an Official, its number must be lower than the previous top card.
  • The action performed must match the symbol on the left of the ability card.

Note: For the first Court Action of a round, the active player may choose any value for the nonexistent previous top card X.
Note: The Move Officials action does not trigger card abilities.
Important! Abilities in a province at maximum unrest do not activate.

Unification Reward Phase

After every player has used all cards, the Play Cards phase ends and the Unification Reward phase begins.
Resolve the reward for writing first, then currency, and finally laws. Each reward goes to the player who contributed most in that field.
The exact reward depends on the round and the type of unification.

First, every player totals the value of their writing-unification tokens. The player with the highest total decides whether to accept that round’s reward:

  • If they accept, the player returns all ivory unification tokens, gains the reward, and moves their disc onto that reward.
  • If they decline, the player keeps the ivory tokens and does not move their disc. After that player passes, the player with the second-highest total decides whether to accept.

Resolve the currency- and law-unification rewards in the same way.

If players tie for the highest total value of unification tokens, determine their priority as follows:
First come players still on the reward track, from top to bottom, followed by players who have already taken a reward in the current round, again from top to bottom.
In the example below, the order is blue, yellow, red, then purple.

After resolving all three fields, return the player discs to the reward track in the order above. The player at the top becomes the new first player.
Note: Unification tokens score points at the end of the game, as explained below.

Tasks: the task area is on the right side of the game board. When a player meets the conditions of a task, they place one disc on the leftmost unoccupied space of that task.
Note: Tasks score points at the end of the game, as explained below.

End of the Game

The game ends after five rounds. Players calculate victory points as follows:

  1. In each province, the player with the most governors receives a base of 9 points plus 3 points for every governor there. If several players tie for the most, they divide these points equally.
  2. The players with the most unification tokens receive 5, 6, or 7 points for the three respective types. Ties are broken by the current disc order.
  3. Score the two task sections separately.
    Add the values beneath a player’s discs.
    Multiply that total by the number of the player’s discs in the section: ×2 for two completed tasks or ×3 for three.
  4. Score points according to the wall tiles.

Variant Rules

  1. In rounds 1–4, players draw three cards from each deck, keep two, and shuffle the discarded cards into the bottom of the relevant deck.
  2. The order on the reward track determines player order instead of clockwise order.
  3. Increase a province’s unrest by 1 for each worker hired there.

Strategy Tips

One of the most important considerations is balancing the number of cards played to your personal board against those played to the game board.
The more cards you place in your personal board, the more powerful your Court Actions become, but you also have fewer opportunities to trigger those abilities.
For a first game, we recommend placing three or four cards in your player board during the first round before taking Court Actions.
Likewise, if you plan to activate card abilities, pay attention to the colors of cards in other players’ hands. For example, if you want to activate abilities linked to Build a Palace, it is better to save high-numbered cards rather than place them in your player board, especially while opponents still hold brown cards.
Your goal is to activate as many card abilities as possible, but do not become trapped by that objective. Victory does not require it at all costs. Sometimes you need to perform an action even when it activates no abilities.
Task requirements are also crucial because they determine where you appoint governors and construct walls and palaces. The more of your discs you place within one task section, the more victory points you receive. We recommend concentrating on one section.
