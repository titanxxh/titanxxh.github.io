[titanxxh Plays Chinese Paladin 5] 4. Combat Review

titanxxh2011-09-182026-08-23

This article has nothing to do with the story. It is only about how the combat feels.

Ordinary enemies are not strong—at least they are weaker than those in Chinese Paladin 4—but some maps contain one unusually powerful minor enemy, such as that snake in Thunderbolt Wasteland...

The boss fights are much more interesting to discuss. If you do not grind, but clear nearly every monster on each map and keep your equipment up to date, bosses in the early and middle portions are fairly easy. They may take a long time, but I can swallow potions and the boss cannot heal, so its defeat is only a matter of time.

The monkeys at the beginning cannot be defeated. The bosses that follow—including the Magic Ape King, Shangguan Ya, and Blood Hand—are not difficult; they merely have a lot of health.

Ice Qilin is probably the first challenging one. It enters an enraged state, so watch your health when frozen. It is still relatively manageable.

By this point I had worked out a low-level boss strategy: consume medicine to build energy, then unleash ultimate attacks. As long as you can survive the incoming damage, you will eventually get a chance to turn defense into offense.

The next few bosses are not especially difficult either. Against a single target, win the war of attrition; against several, eliminate the softest target first.

Ouyang Hui is a hurdle because the protagonist must duel her alone. She is holding back, however, and if you pay attention to her fire spells you can still win. Those lightning orbs are annoying.

I recommend losing the sparring match against Young Master Long, since you can synthesize Eight Fiends yourself anyway.

Feng Gui was the first boss I found genuinely infuriating. It healed itself nine times and nearly exhausted my entire medicine supply, although it was not particularly dangerous.

If you are underleveled against the Bone Snake, bring Xiaoman. The Five-Poison Pearl provides excellent protection against poison; let the men handle the damage output.

The Three Monsters of Mount Huang are delightful, but their damage is no joke. The brush spray is single-target and can easily kill someone outright.

When you challenge Qiongwu again, the balance has shifted dramatically: its dragon attributes have fallen a lot while the heroes have gained levels, making the fight very easy.

The successive battles against the Four Great Families are easy too. They made me seriously question those families' supposed ability...

Yipin from twenty years ago is unbeatable, and twenty years later he remains unbeatable. Did Yao Xian simply refuse to let players see Li Xiaoyao defeated?

The Seven Sages of Mount Shu challenge is where the game's real difficulty begins. After failing twice against Lingyin, I realized how important buffs were. Otherwise the five Lingyins can chain their turns together; with bad luck, all of them use ultimate attacks and death is unavoidable. One talisman is crucial here: Spell Continuation. Extending a buff by two turns is invaluable, letting you deal more damage while weathering the assault. As usual, target the Lingyin with the easiest element first. I was underleveled, so I had Yurou use her sleep skill to lock down the real Lingyin and keep her from acting. Once you rebuild energy, control her again and prevent the enemy from chaining turns as much as possible.

The second fight against Yipin is unwinnable. The Jingtuo Spirit-Recovery Pill that Caogu gave you earlier is meant for this moment. You can also use an Invisibility Gu to dodge chained actions while applying buffs.

Taiwu! The difficulty rises sharply here, and I was still below the recommended level. If, like me, you enjoy pushing the limit, use plenty of immunity items to protect your fragile characters. The male protagonist can survive, but the other two may not. Equip spell-resistance talismans as well. Staying alive is everything! It took me four or five attempts. The changing elemental attributes are very well designed and genuinely challenging.

Demon Lord Jiang Shili and Li Xiao: kill the giant bird first. If you take too long and barely damage the father before the story trigger, the enemy's two ultimate attacks will certainly wipe you out. Fire immunity is still necessary; otherwise the fight is very dangerous.

Mo Yi, Zhi Su, and Jiu Ye: the two smaller enemies have area-of-effect spells, so immunity should ideally negate one of them; otherwise this battle is difficult too. If Mo Yi copies one of your units, the copy's spell damage is terrible no matter whom it copies and can be ignored. If it copies the protagonist, however, a critical hit against Xiaoman is dangerous. Keep every buff active.

The final boss is also the most outrageous one: the World-Annihilating Qiongbing.

When I fought it, Yunfan was probably level 54, Long You 42, and Xiaoman 53. The boss opened with an ultimate attack; Yunfan was left with only 53 health and the other two were knocked out.

For the first time I wondered whether my level was simply too low. I had not even doubted it during the Taiwu fight. Going out to grind would mean repeating the previous two battles, so I decided to keep trying. A few miraculous tools deserve mention: Heavenly Fragrance Life-Restoring Dew, Invisibility Gu, every throwable item, group mana recovery, Spell Continuation, Focus Spirit, Wind-Commanding Art, Rock-Fortification Spell, group spell barriers, and the ultimate attacks of Xiaoman and Yunfan. Be patient, hope for a little luck, do not surrender, and test the limit.

Even with every buff applied, one ultimate attack can leave Xiaoman hovering between zero and thirty health. It comes down to luck: the fewer times characters die, the more ultimate attacks you can launch and the faster the fight proceeds. Also, once inside the creature's body, throw items. They deal 1,200 more damage there than outside, which is excellent value, and the character inside can use ultimate attacks without danger. Make the most of that opportunity to deal damage.

Then sit back and enjoy the ending.

Overall, late-game combat becomes disconnected from the expected character levels. If you are obsessive like me, try pushing the limit: you will experience the appeal of turn-based combat. Which move each person should use, whether to take medicine, and the shape of the action bar are all critical decisions. If someone dies, revive them immediately; their position on the action bar is not pushed back, which is worth remembering. Early bosses merely have too much health and offer little novelty, but the later battles are very well designed and challenging.
