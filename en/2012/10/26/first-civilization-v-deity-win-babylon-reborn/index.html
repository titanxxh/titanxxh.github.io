My First Civilization V Deity Win: The Rebirth of Babylon

titanxxh2012-10-262026-08-22

I recently read many expert guides and game reports on CivFanatics China. After several attempts, this novice finally beat Deity difficulty.

Before moving up in difficulty, I always felt that the AI developed unbelievably fast and I could never keep pace. Then I read I-Da's guide to a beeline for Archery, together with the new expansion's strategy of throwing successive Great Generals at the enemy. It seemed that there might still be a way to catch up.

The main idea is as follows:

Research Composite Bowmen as quickly as possible. Upgrade six Archers and use the brief advantage of the new unit type to overwhelm and eliminate one AI. Then research Philosophy, rush the National College, and beeline along the lower branch of the technology tree. Once Crossbowmen arrive, exploit another temporary advantage to eliminate a second opponent. At that point you control three capitals, wonders included, and have established an advantage large enough that you should be able to win.

The strategy exploits the fact that although the AI develops at divine speed, it researches technologies evenly without a clear focus. A human player can prioritize military technologies and create a sudden burst of strength by upgrading an entire unit class at once, using war to establish an advantage and gradually convert it into victory. The plan looked simple, clear, and thoroughly plausible, so I immediately tested it in two games. I abandoned both halfway through. In the first, I failed to eliminate anyone within eighty turns and gave up. In the second, I destroyed one opponent but fell too far behind militarily in the middle game. I had also pursued Industrialization before Machinery, decided the position was hopeless, and abandoned that game as well. I returned to the reports and found one describing a Babylonian science victory. Babylon's free early Great Scientist and the burst of research from planting an Academy seemed capable of reaching Machinery before the AI, so I tried again. As expected, the Great Scientist awarded after Writing created a scientific lead. After eliminating one opponent, I challenged Sweden, the universal enemy. But by the time I had finally driven Sweden from history, Austria had built a huge empire through diplomatic marriages and had already completed the Apollo Program. Faraway Empress Wu continued developing in peace, grew stronger by the day, and soon completed Apollo as well. After weighing the options repeatedly, I decided to eliminate the nearer Austrian empire first. Fate refused to cooperate: after I captured several Austrian cities, the great Empress had already launched her spaceship. Careful reflection revealed the key cause of failure: happiness management. Continuous conquest left my empire unhappy for most of the middle and late game, severely harming production. Yesterday I started over, again as Babylon. From the beginning, I prioritized happiness, kept it above five, founded new cities only then, avoided trading luxuries carelessly, and bought the friendship of one mercantile city-state. I withstood a powerful assault from the Iroquois Mohawk Warriors, a unique swordsman that requires no iron, coming from the north. Then I immediately sent my first Great General to establish a citadel and made the Iroquois my first target. I had to avenge the humiliation, especially since they had declared war on me; extracting a war indemnity seemed very possible. After sacrificing one Great General to stabilize the defense around my first expansion city, I used the second aggressively, captured the capital Onondaga, and—just as expected—the AI offered reparations and peace on the next turn. I gladly accepted.

Meanwhile, my second and third expansion cities were ready, and I was eyeing the powerful Austria next door. Why is it always Austria? Just as I prepared to attack, the AI married the city-state of Hanoi and blocked my route. I had no choice but to wait for Artillery and capture Hanoi first. That created another problem: after I took Hanoi, Austria attacked on two fronts, sending another force against my third city and giving me a considerable headache. I reloaded a save from several turns earlier and decided to drive between my second and third cities, abandoning the original route south through Hanoi. The hills here made movement difficult, but they also made it hard for the AI to approach. Machine Guns stood behind the Artillery, ready to destroy any enemy that climbed onto a hill. The position supported both attack and defense.

Capturing the vital city of Salzburg effectively opened the roads toward both the Austrian capital and India. Austria still led in score, but India had the technological lead and had completed the Apollo Program. The situation was nevertheless better than in the previous game because the distant Celts and Ethiopia trailed me scientifically and posed no concern. My immediate goals were to devote everything to science, unlock aircraft as quickly as possible, and capture Vienna fast enough to force peace. After planting a Great General's citadel, I took Vienna and secured the most important resource of all: oil. I devoted production to aircraft in preparation for an invasion of India. Artillery captured the frontline city of Vijayanagara and trapped seven early Indian aircraft there. The AI counterattacked with everything it had, and the city changed hands repeatedly. A metropolis that had once contained more than thirty citizens became a ruin with a population of one. When our Knights captured the city, their excellent mobility let them move back out, whereas enemy Infantry were trapped and destroyed inside. After several exchanges, the AI had no hope of retaking the city. My aircraft redeployed to attack Delhi.

The capture of Delhi and Mumbai sealed India's defeat. From then on, aircraft and tanks ruled the world. I rapidly fought my way to the gates of Ethiopia and achieved victory.

Looking back over the entire game, I managed happiness much better. The graph below shows that it was almost never negative. The traditional four-city build, together with circuses, colosseums, and theaters, deserves much of the credit. Later, I also captured many AI wonders that increased happiness.

PS: At the end, the strongest military still belonged not to me but to Austria. Good grief.
