A Game of Thrones Session Report: July 4, 2012

titanxxh2012-07-062026-08-23

As the old saying goes, what has long been united must divide, and what has long been divided must unite. After years of peace, hidden troubles had begun to stir within the realm. The old king's death meant that Westeros would once again be drenched in blood. This time, six kings rose at once to take part in the game of thrones.

They were:

From Dragonstone, the yellow crowned-stag House Baratheon, beginning with the Iron Throne—the symbol of power and order. Regent of the realm and former lord of the Storm Kingdom: Xiaocheng Baratheon.

From Pyke, the black and ferocious kraken of House Greyjoy, beginning with the Valyrian Steel Blade—the symbol of war and strength. Lord of the captains of Ironman's Bay: titanxxh Greyjoy.

From Lannisport, the red and cunning lion of House Lannister, beginning with the Messenger Raven—the symbol of intelligence and intrigue. Warden of the West and lord of the Rock: Zihan Lannister.

From Winterfell, the white direwolf of the frozen north, Warden of the North and lord of the Northern Kingdom: Brother D Stark.

From Highgarden, the green golden rose of House Tyrell, Warden of the South and lord of the Reach: Ji'er Tyrell.

From Sunspear, the orange sun pierced by a spear, lord of Dorne: Xiaoshou Martell.

The rulers of Houses Lannister, Tyrell, and Stark were all newly crowned kings who had not been tested in the previous war.

At the council before hostilities began, the six kings swore together before the old gods and the new: whoever first controlled seven strongholds or castles would receive the immediate submission of the others, who would lower their banners and return their blades to storage. If ten years passed first, they would likewise cease burdening the people with war and acclaim as Protector of the Realm whoever commanded the broadest lands and richest cities.

I will recount the war from my own perspective, that of House Greyjoy. The black kraken began with unmatched military power and followed its family words, “We Do Not Sow.” In the very first year, I sent this letter:

Honorable Lord Lannister,

Our two houses possess the richest lands on the continent, and we are also its closest neighbors. I hope you will not believe the provocations of others. I promise not to attack you first. In exchange, I propose that you surrender Riverrun. If necessary, I am willing to return it when you stand on the verge of victory. Furthermore, if Tyrell or Baratheon dares invade your territory, I will support your battle without condition. May the red and black houses remain friends for generations and resist every foreign foe together.

Respectfully, titanxxh Greyjoy, ruler of Pyke and Ironman's Bay and King in the North—yes, all the same person.

The expected bargaining soon began, but it remained within an acceptable range. Red and black finally agreed that once Lannister controlled five castles, red could begin an attack on Riverrun. My private thought: heh, if you ever reach five, everyone else will be attacking you and you will probably have no attention to spare for Riverrun.

Meanwhile, at the southern end of the continent, Tyrell and Martell went to war as though bound by an ancient feud. Their conflict had little effect on me, but it benefited red and yellow and helped strengthen the red-black friendship by persuading Lannister that the south held more exciting opportunities.

Xiaocheng abandoned his usual opening of marching directly on King's Landing. He chose to muster at his home area, apparently hoping to exploit the white newcomer's mistake and seize the Narrow Sea. Unfortunately for him, I had already warned white about that strategic position. Stark decisively mustered at home and moved troops into the Narrow Sea, thwarting Xiaocheng's scheme.

I used our agreement to advance into Riverrun and Seagard. I deliberately did not march into Flint's Finger because an opening that appeared too dominant would provoke universal hostility.

In years two and three, the Westeros cards pleased me by producing supply and mustering, giving me four supply and the greatest recruitment capacity. What did not please me were two unexpectedly timed Clash of Kings events—one chosen by red. I failed to retain the Valyrian Steel Blade, which instead went to my direct enemy Stark. I remained last on the Raven track, and the tragedy of having no starred orders kept me from advancing quickly into Bay of Ice. My private thought: this was my best opportunity to pacify the north in a single move, but I failed to seize it and gave Stark room to breathe.

Green overextended aggressively. The Raven position he had just obtained was pushed to last by Doran Martell. Orange and green continued grinding one another down and lost their early advantage. House Baratheon profited and soon became everyone's target, drawing red into an effort to contain him. At this point my strategy had partly succeeded and partly failed. Riverrun remained alert for a Lannister betrayal, while Ironman's Bay increased its shipbuilding to secure the seas.

My awkward position on the influence tracks and lack of starred orders convinced me to consolidate political power and temporarily avoid war, hoping to win the Blade and Raven when the next Clash of Kings appeared. I also built two siege engines at home, intending to seize a better turn order and then take Winterfell directly. Heaven did not cooperate: the second Clash of Kings did not arrive until the third-to-last round, but that comes later. In the meantime I repeatedly probed white's Bay of Ice, hoping to draw out Eddard Stark. I failed, though at least I removed Roose Bolton and Robb Stark.

Green and orange eventually made peace after witnessing yellow's strength. Unfortunately, both had already committed their best commanders and could do little, so red became the principal force preventing a Baratheon victory. I kept my promise never to invade red territory, even when doing so would have been easier. My private thought: I also wanted to avoid distracting red from checking yellow and allowing yellow to grow unchecked. Nor did I want to become conspicuously powerful too soon. Red's lands were easy for others to surround, while white's were much easier to defend.

The radius of time's wheel is proportional to the scale of the war, and each revolution became slower than the last.

In the eighth year, Clash of Kings finally appeared, but circumstances had changed. The expected great war in the central lands had not erupted. Every house possessed increasing stores of power, while I had entered the awkward position of being unable to gain any. Forced by necessity, I spent every power token to accept rankings of second, third, and third on the three influence tracks.

Even without the Blade, starred orders helped me surge into Bay of Ice and capture Winterfell through three linked attacks. With Balon in hand, I could guarantee holding it until the commanders returned. But calculations cannot defeat fate. Clash of Kings appeared again in round nine, while every player simultaneously realized that black's momentum was becoming unstoppable. The treacherous red lion broke our alliance: Gregor Clegane brutally killed three of my knights and retook Riverrun. Lannister also incited Tyrell to use the Queen of Thorns to remove my starred support order in Bay of Ice.

The balance shifted in an instant and left me dangerously exposed. At that critical moment, I voluntarily returned Winterfell to white so he would not need to spend Eddard Stark. I counterattacked Riverrun, killed red's two siege engines, and stabilized my rear while temporarily easing my conflict with white. Pointing out that yellow held the Iron Throne, Valyrian Steel Blade, and Messenger Raven, I tried to redirect everyone's attention and coax green, orange, and yellow into fighting among themselves.

The final year arrived at last. A starred Lannister support order in Blackwater became the key to the entire contest. Castles around the Blackwater changed hands repeatedly. At the decisive moment, the red lion displayed a little honor and, following a principle of aiding the weak, held green, orange, and yellow to four castles each. I concentrated my forces, relied on King Balon and the Iron Captain to capture and hold Winterfell, and likewise reached four castles. Because I controlled three strongholds, I won!!!

The ten-year struggle among six kings ended. Every house had endured political contests, diplomatic maneuvering, and military confrontation. In the end, House Greyjoy proved that taking by force surpassed laboring in the fields. With iron and blood, it defended the honor of the ironborn, ascended the Iron Throne, and became the second king and Protector of the Realm.
